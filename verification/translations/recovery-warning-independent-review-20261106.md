## Dzongkha: holds resolved by primary grammar

Both new possible-state clauses use `ཨིནམ་འོང` instead of the earlier less-grounded composition. The exported notice uses `ཡོདཔ་འོང` for ancillary presence, followed by the separate negative inspection clause `ཨིན་རུང་ དེ་ཚུ་ཞིབ་དཔྱད་མ་འབད།`.

Independently read Tshering and van Driem's [The Grammar of Dzongkha, 2019](https://escholarship.org/content/qt1h4211k0/qt1h4211k0_noSplash_b3843a79888f78f39713ded5f61ad772.pdf?t=s10u2j), printed pp.244–245, 310, 329, 333–334, in the downloaded primary PDF. It distinguishes present/past potentiality from future certainty, gives the two relevant inflected copula/existence forms, defines past negative `ma-`, demonstrates the inspection verb, and supports sentence-initial adversative usage. These are grammatical components, not an attested software sentence. No assertion that the earlier inherited damage term is newly wrong or newly accepted is made.

Full new-warning reading preserves: unchanged raw export; possible damage/unreadability; the joint ZIP/readable-canvas condition; extraction and the actual localized load route; unverified optional settings/selection; reason and separate retained copy; resizing only afterwards; retention even if unopenable. The negative inspection sentence no longer risks implying inspection occurred but yielded no assurance.

## Okinawan: holds resolved with targeted potential forms

Each new warning has exactly three changes:
- `読み取ららん` → `読まらん`, conditional inability to read.
- `読み取いる` → `読まりーる`, explicit readable-potential adnominal.
- `開きらりてぃ、` → `開きらりーねー、`, explicit ZIP-opening condition, followed by the existing readable-canvas existence condition. The extraction instruction is inside both nested conditions.

The final `開きららん` is retained. Its weak opening-verb stem differs from the strong reading verb, so mechanically applying the reading negative to both would be wrong. The correction does not use the learned-skill ability construction as a substitute for file readability.

Evidence and exact retrieval limits:
- [Hanazono's 2014 introductory Okinawan textbook proposal](https://www.tufs.ac.jp/icjs/images/publications/journal_004.pdf), printed119–121/PDF123–125, downloaded and independently read as text. It separates ordinary adnominals, learned-skill ability, conditional possibility, condition/concession and not-yet forms. The grammar provides compositional paradigms, not modern file-recovery terminology.
- [Shuri verb morphology paper](https://u-ryukyu.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/2007908/files/jinken25text.pdf), indexed printed51–52 excerpts explicitly distinguish the reading stem's potential form from ordinary reading and learned skill. Fresh PDF retrieval returned503 and the web opening429, so this part remains indexed primary evidence, not a claimed complete PDF or visual inspection.
- [JLect's directly inspected reproduction of the NINJAL Okinawan dictionary opening entry](https://www.jlect.com/search.php?group=words&l=ryukyu&r=akiyuN) gives transitive opening and its `=raN` negative stem. This is not the separate intransitive dawn sense.
- [JLect's directly inspected reproduction of Miyara's practical dictionary](https://www.jlect.com/search.php?group=words&l=ryukyu&r=kuNda) contains a conditional-inability example about an illegible signature, supporting `ゆまらんたん`. The neighboring Yaeyama/Hatoma section was excluded.
- [TUFS paper on the Okinawan passive/potential auxiliary](https://www.tufs.ac.jp/ts/personal/kazama/shigen/10/Shigen10.pdf) supplies indexed `らりーねー` conditional corroboration. Direct opening returned403, so no complete-page or screenshot claim is made.

The complete warning remains an authored functional composition in the established Shuri/Naha-oriented register. The conditional forms are source-supported morphology; the precise full digital sentence has not been speaker-certified. Japanese-derived technical nouns are not rejected solely for being loans. No other existing Okinawan values were changed or reopened.

- **Ainu Latin, both: accept, moderate compositional confidence.** `nankor` marks conjecture rather than a confirmed diagnosis; the coordinated ZIP-opening/readable-canvas antecedent ends in `yakun`; `yakka` preserves keeping an unopenable copy; `naa ... ka somo ki` denies completed examination. The copy, inside contents and extracted image are kept distinct. Computer terms remain contextual extensions of existing language, not historically attested software phrases.

- **Tibetan, both: accept, moderate-to-good semantic confidence.** Possibility `ཡིན་སྲིད`/`ཡོད་སྲིད`, conjunctive antecedent ending `ཡོད་ན`, explicit `ཞིབ་བཤེར་བྱས་མེད`, and `ཀྱང` retain the required boundaries. - **Jeju, both: accept, moderate compositional confidence.** Completed export/retention, current possibility, the ZIP plus readable image condition in `이시민`, `안허엿수다` negative verification, and `열 수 엇어도` concession are distinguishable. Shared Korean technical nouns are legitimate context, not falsely advertised dialect-specific coinages. NIKL supports connective/aspect distinctions, not every candidate word or a fluent-speaker review.
- **Hakka PFS, both: correct final concession, otherwise accept at moderate confidence.** `Yì` can represent 如 and is not inherently erroneous; `sat-thin`, `fe̍t`, and `thu̍k` are documented PFS forms. MOE directly illustrates 還吂確定, supporting `hàn-màng khok-thin`. The less secure `Chhiu-sṳ́` concession should be replaced with the already supported conditional `Yì ... ya oi ...` ('if it cannot open, also keep...'). The two safety conditions stay conjoined by `lâu`; source-based technical loans are not rejected solely because their Chinese cognates exist.
- **Traditional Mongolian preserved: accept, moderate confidence.** `kerbe ... baibal` places both ZIP ability and readable image under the antecedent; optional resizing follows the image-load instruction; inability-to-open concession does not authorize discarding.
- **Traditional Mongolian exported: correct the verification predicate, then accept, moderate confidence.** The original `silgaju batulaga ügei` combines 'checking' with absence of the noun assurance/evidence. Use an explicit negated checking verb rather than imply checked but unconfirmed. All possibility, unchanged-content and keep-copy clauses otherwise preserve scope. Vertical long-dialog layout is untested.
- **Manchu, both: correct opening conditional, then accept, moderate confidence.** Change `neime mutefi` to `neime muteci`: -fi is the past converb ('having/after'), while -ci makes ZIP-opening ability explicitly conditional; the following readable-canvas `bici` remains a second condition. `ainci` qualifies possible damage and ancillary presence; `baicahakū` denies examination. These are modern descriptive extensions, not historical computing terminology. Do not infer all byte contents are an image merely from the existing recovery-copy label.
- **Vietnamese Chữ Nôm, both: correct extraction glyph/phrase, then accept, moderate confidence.** The Vietnamese reading preserves explicit conditional extraction and keeping even an unopenable copy. `閧` has the Nôm reading hỏng, so Chinese-only meanings would be a wrong rejection basis. However, `搌` is supported as nện, not nén; replace `giải nện` with a supported font-safe extraction paraphrase. Verify every replacement glyph against the actual bundled font cmap. Cmap coverage does not certify meaning or shaping. Nôm orthographic variation remains.
- **Shan, both: accept, moderate compositional confidence.** The modal `ၸၢင်ႈ` can govern the coordinated damage/unreadability alternatives. `သင် ... လႄႈ ... ၼႆ` places ZIP opening and readable-canvas existence jointly under the condition; the explicit not-yet predicate denies checking/confirmation. The keep-copy concession and after-that optional resize are preserved. An extra repeated modal is not required merely because the full sentence is newly authored.
